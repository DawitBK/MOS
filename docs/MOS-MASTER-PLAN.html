Manufacturing Operations Suite · planning package v0.3 · 27 Sep 2026

MOS Master Plan

A company-neutral ERP for running a plant: procurement with supplier payment, inventory, raw-material lab, production, sales and gate control. v1 runs as one installation per company and delivers all six modules in six months of development, then two months to go live. The headline loop: a truck is weighed, the lab grades and accepts the load, the goods receipt is prepared automatically and the supplier payment process starts.

What changed in v0.3. The owner re-framed the plan, and a line-by-line audit of v0.2 recorded 135 findings, each resolved or assigned to a later phase:
  1. One installation per company, no tenancy in v1, with seams to grow into multi-company and SaaS later (ADR-0017).
  2. All six modules in v1: ≤ 6 months of development, then 2 months to go live, with three team paths to choose from (ADR-0018).
  3. No infrastructure assumed: hosting is decided after the stakeholders answer (ADR-0019).
  4. The client is a flour mill: its draft inspects durum wheat for “Addis Flour Factory”. The procure-to-pay swimlane was the owner’s ChatGPT drawing, not the client’s.
  5. Production, sales and receivables are designed for v1; three design defects that would have failed in production are fixed.
Details: MOS-PACKAGE-AUDIT-v0.3.md.
Scope

Six modules, one ledger

Procurement + payment

Supplier registration (5 steps), requisitions with live stock, purchase orders, goods receipt, payment requests, approvals, cheque/transfer payments.

Inventory

Warehouses, stores and silos; stock by batch and status; transfers, adjustments, counts, silo dip measurement, moving-average value.

Lab & quality

Versioned specs, inspection before unloading or in quarantine, grades and quality score, decisions, deduction and grade price rules.

Production

Recipes with co- and by-products, milling runs with extraction rate, packing into bag sizes, finished-goods release, output costing.

Sales

Customers, credit checks, orders, deliveries with outbound weighing, invoicing with fiscal reference, receipts, returns.

Gate

Vehicles and goods in and out, manual weigh tickets with strong controls, exit control, gate passes, paper fallback.

Plus a payables/receivables sub-ledger that exports journals to the customer’s existing accounting package. MOS is not a general ledger.

Honest assessment

What to keep in mind

  • Six months for six modules works only at lean depth, and without cuts only on path A. The client should see the v1.1 and Later lists before signing.
  • Production and sales are designed from flour-milling practice, not yet from the client’s own description. Workshops before M4 and M5 are essential.
  • The client’s draft has about 40 defects (e.g. the payment request reuses the PR- prefix, status fields are editable, the stock example counts rejection and damage twice). The plan builds the corrected versions.
  • AI speeds up coding, not decisions. Client answers, UAT, data migration and hosting take calendar time, which is why the go-live window is two months.

1.0Production
0.75Sales

Foundation carries the services every module needs from its first document (approvals, numbering, attachments, PDFs, notifications). The inventory ledger starts in parallel in M1 because every posting goes through it. QC and gate are narrow but control-heavy. Production is the most domain-specific. Sales reuses what came before. The reserve is explicit because the go-live window can’t double as build buffer.

New in v0.3

What the audit added

  • Flour-mill realities: bagged receipts with bag tare, silo dip measurement, fumigation holds, milling runs with grist, tempering water and extraction rate, packing into bag sizes, optional fortification.
  • Money details: gross / VAT / WHT / net on payment requests, cheque books with gap detection, post-dated cheques, company paying accounts (bank, cash, mobile), tax registers, self-billing tax rules, individual no-TIN suppliers.
  • Rules made precise: how deductions combine, the quality-score formula, receipt valuation, one rule for invoices above payable quantity, SoD restated around the payment request.
  • Client’s own flow: inspection before unloading with the gate module off creates the inspection and prepares the GRN (flow C, AS-13).
  • Sales essentials: fiscal receipt number, customer advances, cash sessions, deliveries creating expected gate visits.
  • A canonical status vocabulary across all documents, and six new acceptance scenarios for production and sales (AS-14…19).

Architecture · design only

A modular monolith with the database as the safety net

One API and one worker share one PostgreSQL database, with one schema per module in strict dependency tiers. Immutability and ledger invariants are enforced in the database (insert-only grants, a guard trigger, CHECK constraints), not only in code. The queue and file storage sit behind ports, so the hosting decision doesn’t change the code.

What it taught us

Signals in the draft

  • Flour mill: the inspection screen names “Addis Flour Factory” and durum wheat; the stock screen lists bran.
  • Bagged wheat: 450 bags = 225 quintals = 22,500 kg, so bag tare and empty-bag handling matter.
  • Production already touches procurement: “Issue to Production Line 1” and a bran receipt appear in the stock history.
  • Supplier self-registration may be expected: the wizard is titled “Supplier Portal – New Registration”.

Controls

Built in, not bolted on

  • Segregation of duties: the PO creator never posts its GRN; neither may approve a payment request covering them; the requester can’t approve it and the approver can’t record the payment; lab analysts can’t approve concessions on their own results; weighbridge operators can’t verify their own weights.
  • Bank details: never edited in place; independent approval, call-back and cooling-off; payment blocked if details changed after approval; method fixed at approval.
  • Exit control: no outbound goods leave without a posted document and a weight within tolerance. Not overridable.
  • Money trail: gap-free receipts, payments, weigh tickets, invoices and cheque books; voids kept; proof of payment attached.
  • Immutability: posted records, ledgers, QC results and approval history can’t be edited; corrections are reversals; a sealed audit log.
